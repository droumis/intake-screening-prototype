# Reference Material — Colorado Regulatory Context

**Document type:** reference_material
**Fabricated:** Written for the PISA prototype as illustrative background. Not verified clinical or legal reference material.
**Purpose:** Screening-level background and citations for the Colorado dataset. No dosing guidance; medication decisions belong to prescribers and the clearance pathways.

## Primary sources

- **C.R.S. Article 170 / Natural Medicine Health Act** (Proposition 122, 2022) and **SB23-290** (implementing statute).
- **4 CCR 755-1** — DORA, Office of Natural Medicine Licensure rules: facilitator scope of practice (Rule 2.2), safety screen (Rule 2.5), conduct, touch consent, records.
- **1 CCR 212-3** — Department of Revenue, Natural Medicine Division rules: healing centers, administration areas, servings, handling.
- DORA Natural Medicine Division program pages; Psychedelic Alpha's "Rundown of Colorado's Natural Medicine Program" for a current overview.

## Key regulatory facts for screeners

- **No categorical participant exclusions.** Colorado's model converts risk into documentation: risk factors on the safety screen mean the facilitator may not proceed **independently** — services may continue only with a documented referral, medical clearance, or consultation-and-risk-review, plus a written safety plan where heightened risk is identified. A Colorado screening flag's resolution criteria should name the pathway, not declare ineligibility.
- **Lithium and antipsychotics** are the strictest medication tier: the clearance/consultation requirement applies even for Clinical Facilitators unless prescribing those medications is within their own secondary scope. Contrast Oregon, where lithium in the last 30 days is a flat legal exclusion.
- **Other psychotropic medications** identified on the screen trigger a required **written recommendation** to obtain consultation.
- **Facilitator types matter:** standard Facilitators may not practice medicine or psychotherapy and may not independently serve participants with active risk-factor diagnoses; **Clinical Facilitators** (dual-licensed clinicians) may treat conditions within their secondary scope. Screening output should include routing.
- **Session mechanics:** 21+; servings ≤10 mg total psilocin, containers ≤50 mg, with the number of servings per session set by participant–facilitator agreement (no fixed statutory session cap); minimum durations scale with dose (≥1 hour at ≤2 mg to ≥5 hours at the upper range); group sessions capped at 1 facilitator : 4 participants above 2 mg total psilocin (1:8, up to 64 participants, for microdose-only groups) — operational rules, not eligibility screening.
- **Locations:** licensed healing centers plus other authorized locations (including private residences and healthcare facilities under additional parameters).
- **In-session duties:** continuous assessment for distress and suicidality; touch only under a prior written consent agreement limited to hands, feet, shoulders; others present in the administration area require each participant's prior written consent.
- **Attestations:** participants attest in writing to the accuracy of health information, informed consent, and touch-consent parameters.

## Clinical background (house-criteria basis)

- Lithium + classical psychedelics: case literature associates the combination with seizures and severe adverse events — why R-3 demands the full pathway plus a safety plan, and why house criteria stay conservative even where the law allows clearance.
- MAOIs: interaction risk; discontinuation is prescriber-managed — house H-5.
- Psychosis and bipolar spectrum: recurrence/episode-trigger risk — house H-3/H-4 exceed the regulatory framework deliberately.
- Cardiovascular: transient HR/BP elevation during sessions — H-6/H-8.
- SSRIs/SNRIs: commonly attenuated response; never advise dose changes (R-4 recommendation route).
- Serotonergic supplements (St. John's Wort, passionflower) and grapefruit: interaction/load considerations; discontinuation windows set at intake.
