# Reference Material: Fasting, Altitude, Cold Exposure — Interaction Notes

**Document type:** reference_material
**Fabricated:** Written for the PISA prototype as illustrative background. Not verified clinical or legal reference material.
**Purpose:** Curated background knowledge for the screening assistant. This replaces live research in v1. Screeners should verify anything uncertain with current sources; flags may suggest lookup terms.

## Medications and prolonged fasting (48h)

| Medication / class | Concern during a 48h fast with exertion | Screening implication |
|---|---|---|
| Lithium | Narrow therapeutic index; dehydration and sodium shifts raise serum levels; toxicity risk | Exclusionary (criterion A1) |
| Diuretics (HCTZ, furosemide) | Compound dehydration and electrolyte loss | Physician must address explicitly (C1) |
| ACE inhibitors / ARBs (lisinopril, losartan) | Generally tolerated; hypotension risk increases when fasted and dehydrated; monitor for dizziness | Physician clearance (C1) |
| Beta-blockers (metoprolol, propranolol) | Blunt heart-rate response; mask overexertion; can worsen cold extremities during immersion | Prescriber clearance for these activities (C2) |
| SSRIs/SNRIs (sertraline, escitalopram, venlafaxine) | Usually fine to continue; do not skip doses; some cause GI upset without food | Confirm no dose changes planned; prescriber aware (C3) |
| Metformin | Generally held or adjusted during prolonged fasting per prescriber | Physician fast plan required (C4) |
| Insulin | Cannot be safely managed in this setting | Exclusionary (A7) |
| NSAIDs (regular use) | Kidney stress when dehydrated | Follow up on frequency |
| Medications labeled "take with food" | Absorption and GI tolerance issues over 48h | Prescriber plan (C9) |

## Supplements

- **St. John's Wort:** induces CYP3A4 (reduces levels of many drugs) and adds serotonergic load with SSRIs/SNRIs. Always flag when combined with any prescription medication.
- **Grapefruit (regular consumption):** inhibits CYP3A4; raises levels of some statins, calcium channel blockers, and other drugs. Flag alongside any prescription medication; ask applicant to abstain for 72h before and during program per medical advisor guidance.
- **Passionflower:** mild sedative; additive with other sedatives; flag if combined with sleep medications or benzodiazepines.

## Altitude (7,800–10,200 ft)

- Common in first 48h: headache, poor sleep, reduced exercise capacity. Compounded by caffeine withdrawal if the applicant has not tapered.
- Cardiovascular load of exertion is meaningfully higher than at sea level; this is the basis for the cardiac criteria (A4) and beta-blocker caution (C2).
- Sleep disruption at altitude is a recognized trigger for mood episodes in bipolar spectrum conditions (basis for A3/B3).

## Cold-water immersion (45–55°F)

- Cold shock response: gasp reflex, hyperventilation, acute blood pressure and heart rate spike in the first 60–90 seconds. This is the acute cardiac stressor motivating A4.
- Raynaud's phenomenon, cold urticaria: follow up if disclosed.
- Asthma: cold air/water can trigger bronchospasm in susceptible individuals (basis for C5 follow-up).

## Fasting and mental health

- Prolonged fasting affects mood, sleep, and cognition; combined with the day-4 solo period, this is the peak-intensity point of the program.
- Fasting is contraindicated with any eating disorder history (A8): a structured fast can reactivate restrictive patterns even after long recovery; this is why A8 has no time limit.
- Fasting and sleep deprivation each lower seizure threshold (basis for A5).

## Caffeine and cannabis cessation

- Abrupt caffeine cessation: headache, fatigue, irritability for 2–9 days; overlaps with altitude symptoms. Taper guidance: reduce by ~50% per 3–4 days over the two weeks before arrival.
- Cannabis cessation in regular users (≥4x/week): sleep disruption and vivid dreams for 1–2 weeks; compounds altitude sleep effects (basis for D2 follow-up).
