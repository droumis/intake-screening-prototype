# Juniper Ridge — Client Screening Criteria

**Document type:** screening_criteria
**Fabricated:** This organization and these policies are invented test material for the PISA prototype. Not a real organization, not real policy, not clinical or legal advice.
**Version:** 1.4 — reviewed by consulting clinician
**Instruction to screeners:** These criteria reflect the center's license conditions, published clinical screening practice, and the specific demands of a group-format retreat 40 minutes from emergency care. EXCLUSIONARY criteria mean the client should not be accepted into the standard program; an exclusionary criterion may be set aside only with written documentation resolving the specific risk, reviewed by the consulting clinician. CAUTION criteria require follow-up and resolution before clearance. Screeners must never advise a client to alter, taper, skip, or stop any medication; all medication questions are referred to the client's prescriber, and prescriber-directed plans must be documented.

## A. Exclusionary criteria (hard)

- **A1. Lithium therapy.** Any client currently prescribed lithium. Rationale: the combination of lithium and classical psychedelics is associated in the case literature with seizures and severe adverse reactions; this center cannot manage that risk.
- **A2. MAOI antidepressants (e.g., phenelzine, tranylcypromine, selegiline at antidepressant doses).** Rationale: pharmacological interaction risk and unpredictable intensity; requires prescriber-managed discontinuation far beyond this center's scope. No client on an MAOI may be scheduled.
- **A3. Personal history of psychosis at any time.** Includes schizophrenia spectrum disorders, schizoaffective disorder, brief psychotic episode, or any break from reality, however the client frames it (including as a "spiritual emergency" or "awakening"), and regardless of resolution. Rationale: psilocybin can precipitate recurrence; the group format and remote setting cannot contain an emergent episode.
- **A4. Bipolar I disorder, or any manic episode at any time; hypomanic episode within the past 5 years.** Rationale: psilocybin sessions, disrupted routine, and emotional intensity are episode triggers.
- **A5. Suicidal ideation, intent, or attempt within the past 12 months; or self-harm within the past 12 months.** Rationale: sessions reliably surface difficult material; the program removes clients from their usual supports and is not a clinical setting.
- **A6. Unstable cardiovascular disease.** Heart attack or stroke within 12 months; unstable angina; symptomatic or undiagnosed arrhythmia / irregular heart rate; uncontrolled hypertension (untreated, or above 140/90 despite treatment); diagnosed long QT or QT-prolonging condition. Rationale: psilocybin transiently raises heart rate and blood pressure.
- **A7. Seizure history, unless seizure-free 2+ years with documentation.** Rationale: remote setting; some interaction and threshold concerns.
- **A8. Pregnancy or possible pregnancy.**
- **A9. Current untreated substance use disorder, or physiological alcohol or sedative dependence.** Rationale: required abstinence in a setting that cannot manage withdrawal.
- **A10. Allergy to mushrooms or other fungi.** Any prior allergic reaction to consuming mushrooms/fungi is exclusionary pending allergist documentation.
- **A11. Under the legal minimum age for psilocybin services in this jurisdiction.**

## B. Caution criteria — mental and psychological (follow-up required before clearance)

- **B1. First-degree relative with a psychotic or bipolar I disorder.** Requires consulting-clinician review before scheduling. (Second-degree relatives or unclear family accounts: note and discuss at intake.)
- **B2. Trauma history without current therapeutic support.** Sessions can surface trauma material vividly; integration support is expected. Resolution: current or newly established therapeutic relationship, or an intake conversation establishing adequate support and a concrete integration plan.
- **B3. Dissociative symptoms.** Depersonalization, derealization, significant memory gaps, or pronounced "checking out," especially under stress. Follow up on frequency, severity, triggers; escalate to the consulting clinician if episodes are frequent or uncontrolled.
- **B4. Hypomanic episode more than 5 years ago, or bipolar II / cyclothymia.** Consulting-clinician review plus prescriber letter required.
- **B5. Major depressive disorder or anxiety disorder, currently treated and stable.** Compatible in principle. Requires confirmation of stability (no treatment change in 6 months) and prescriber awareness of the planned session. Watch for treatment-expectation framing (see F4).
- **B6. Panic disorder.** Follow up on frequency, triggers, and management, with specific attention to the no-early-exit nature of a many-hour session and the group setting.
- **B7. Any deferred, skipped, or "let's discuss in person" answer in the mental health, trauma, sexuality, or substance use sections.** Always follow up in the intake conversation; never treat as a negative answer. Deferred answers in the sexuality section are handled with particular care and privacy, one-on-one, per the form's own guidance.
- **B8. Recent major loss or life crisis (bereavement, divorce, job loss within 6 months).** Follow up on stability and timing.
- **B9. History of a significantly difficult psychedelic experience.** Not disqualifying by itself; follow up on what happened, what support existed, how it resolved, and what was learned. Escalate if the account includes psychotic features (then A3 applies).

## C. Caution criteria — medical and medications (follow-up required before clearance)

- **C1. SSRI/SNRI antidepressants (e.g., sertraline, escitalopram, venlafaxine).** Not exclusionary. Required follow-ups: (a) confirm the client will not alter dosing except under prescriber direction, and document any prescriber-directed plan; (b) confirm prescriber awareness of the planned session; (c) set expectations that response intensity may differ for clients on these medications; (d) screen for concurrent serotonergic supplements (see C4).
- **C2. Tricyclic antidepressants.** Consulting-clinician review required before scheduling.
- **C3. Controlled hypertension on medication.** Requires written prescriber clearance addressing the transient heart-rate and blood-pressure rise of a psilocybin session. Note grapefruit interactions with several blood-pressure medications (see C5).
- **C4. Serotonergic or interaction-prone supplements.** St. John's Wort (serotonergic load; broad interactions) and passionflower (mild MAO-related activity and sedative load) must be discontinued ahead of the session per intake plan — this is why the form asks about both. Follow up on any regular use, especially combined with an SSRI/SNRI.
- **C5. Regular grapefruit consumption.** Interacts with many medications (including some blood-pressure and psychiatric medications). Follow up whenever the client also takes prescription medication; agree an abstention window per the consulting clinician's guidance.
- **C6. Tramadol or other seizure-threshold-lowering / serotonergic analgesics.** Consulting-clinician review; often resolved by a prescriber-directed plan.
- **C7. Benzodiazepines or sedative-hypnotics (regular use).** Consulting-clinician review: dependence assessment (see A9) and session-day plan documented with the prescriber.
- **C8. Diabetes.** Prescriber plan required for the semi-fasted session day, including any medications or monitoring needed during the session (the form asks specifically about session-day medications and devices; record the plan).
- **C9. Asthma or other conditions requiring rescue medication.** Rescue medication must be present in the administration room per the client's session-day medication answer; note triggers.
- **C10. Medications that must be taken with food, or at times that fall inside the session.** Prescriber plan required (the session is many hours and semi-fasted).
- **C11. Mobility, communication, or personal-care support needs.** Not a safety exclusion; triggers the logistics workflow for a client support person per the form's Additional Support section.

## D. Substance use

- **D1. Heavy alcohol use (≥15 drinks/week or daily use):** assess for dependence per A9; if dependence suspected, exclusionary.
- **D2. Cannabis use ≥4 times/week:** follow up on the pre-retreat cessation plan and sleep impact.
- **D3. Any non-prescribed stimulant, opioid, or sedative use within 3 months:** follow up; escalate per A9 as indicated.
- **D4. Nicotine:** not disqualifying; note that clients cannot smoke/vape during the administration session and plan accordingly.

## E. Positive indicators (record as green flags)

- **E1.** Current, ongoing therapeutic relationship, or an integration appointment already scheduled.
- **E2.** Prior psychedelic experience that was adequately supported and well integrated (especially in ceremonial or facilitated contexts).
- **E3.** Established contemplative or somatic practice (meditation, breathwork, bodywork).
- **E4.** Strong, specifically named support network aware of the client's plans.
- **E5.** Realistic goals and expectations; self-aware statement of fears (as opposed to expecting the session to cure a condition or transform them wholesale).
- **E6.** Prescriber clearance or plans already in hand.

## F. Cross-section checks (screeners: look for these explicitly)

- **F1.** Self-described health rating inconsistent with checked conditions, listed medications, or hospitalization history.
- **F2.** A medication whose indication implies a condition not disclosed elsewhere (or vice versa).
- **F3.** Substance quantities in the consumption table inconsistent with narrative answers.
- **F4.** Goals framing the retreat as treatment or cure for a clinical condition ("this will fix my depression"). The program is not medical or clinical treatment; this framing requires a follow-up conversation to reset expectations, and escalation if the client's stability depends on that outcome.
- **F5.** Past psychedelic accounts containing unrecognized red-flag features (loss of contact with reality, persisting perceptual changes, multi-day aftereffects) that the client narrates neutrally.
